Flaps [000S]

  • PPL

  • Plain flap

    • Attached to the wing by a hinge.
    • When deflected downward, it increases the effective camber and changes the wing’s chord line.

  • Split flap

    • Hinged only to the lower portion of the wing.
    • Produces greater drag than plain flap because of the turbulent wake it causes.

  • Slotted flap

    • Similar to plain flap, but it allows a portion of high pressure air beneath the wing to travel through a slot, increasing the velocity of the airflow over the wing and produces additional lift.
    • Delays airflow separation to a higher AoA.

  • Fowler flap

    • Attached to the wing by a track and roller system.
    • When deployed, it moves rearward and down, increasing the total wing area, as well as the camber and chord line.

Flaps produce a large amount of lift for a small increase in drag up to approximately the mid point of flap extension. Once the flaps are extended beyond the midpoint, they will produce a large amount of drag for a small increase in lift.

High-speed airplanes also employ high-lift devices on the leading edge of the wing to improve low-speed performance. Leading-edge flaps increase wing camber. Fixed slots and slats conduct high-energy airflow beneath the wing to the upper surface, delaying airflow separation to a higher AoA.
