Drag [000T]

  • My forest

  • Parasite drag: caused by any aircraft surface that deflects or interferes with the smooth airflow around the airplane.

    • Form drag: results from the turbulent wake caused by the separation of airflow from the surface of a structure.
    • Interference drag: occurs when the airflow around one part of the airplane interacts with the airflow around an adjacent part. Drag results when flows of air moving at different speeds or in different directions combine.
    • Skin friction drag: caused by the roughness of the airplane’s surface.
  • Induced drag: caused by the wingtip vortices, which deflect the airstream in the vicinity of the wingtips downward. The increased downwash moves the average relative wind downward and rearward; thus the average lift is inclined aft by the same amount.

Parasite drag increases proportionally to the square of the airspeed. Assuming the same amount of generated lift, since the wing is at a lower AoA at high speed, induced drag is inversely proportional to the square of the airspeed.

At low speeds, induced drag contributes more to the total drag, while at high speeds, parasite drag dominates.

L/Dmax is the airspeed at which the lift-to-drag ratio is the greatest.
